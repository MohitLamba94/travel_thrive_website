About Amritsar

A city built around a pool of nectar.

Founded in 1577 by the fourth Sikh Guru, Guru Ram Das, Amritsar takes its name from the Amrit Sarovar — the "Pool of Nectar" that surrounds the Golden Temple.

Here faith, food and history sit side by side. Pilgrims and travellers arrive from across the world to walk the marble parkarma, share a meal in the world's largest community kitchen, and witness the fervour of the India–Pakistan border just beyond the city.

Gold by day, glowing reflections by night — Amritsar stays with you.

History & Significance

Faith, empire and the freedom struggle.

Amritsar grew around the sacred sarovar and the Golden Temple (Sri Harmandir Sahib), the holiest shrine in Sikhism. Sitting on historic trade routes, this crossroads position shaped its now-legendary cuisine as travellers brought new ingredients and techniques. The city later flourished as the capital of the Sikh Empire under Maharaja Ranjit Singh.

  • Golden Temple — the founding heart of the city and of the Sikh faith.
  • Jallianwala Bagh — the 1919 massacre, a turning point in India's freedom struggle.
  • Partition of 1947 — memorialised today in the world's first Partition Museum.

Culture & Festivals

Warmth, seva and a full-hearted welcome.

Punjabi hospitality is loud, generous and colourful. The city runs on the spirit of seva (selfless service) and langar — the free community kitchen that feeds everyone, regardless of faith or status. Time your visit around a festival and the whole city lights up.

Baisakhi
~13–14 April, harvest & Khalsa foundation

Best Time to Visit

October to March is the sweet spot.

The pleasant window runs October through March, when the weather is ideal for sightseeing, temple visits and the open-air Wagah ceremony.

Things to Do

The sights that define a visit.

01 · Golden Temple
Sacred landmark

Golden Temple (Sri Harmandir Sahib)

The holiest shrine in Sikhism, its gilded sanctum mirrored in the sacred Amrit Sarovar. The 24-hour langar feeds 40,000+ people a day, free of charge. Utterly magical after dark when the gold glows against the water.

Enquire to visit
02 · Jallianwala Bagh
Historic memorial

Jallianwala Bagh

A solemn memorial garden to the 1919 massacre, with preserved bullet marks and the martyrs' well. A five-minute walk from the Golden Temple, it is a moving reminder of India's freedom struggle.

03 · Wagah Border
Evening spectacle

Wagah–Attari Border

The theatrical evening "Beating Retreat" ceremony at the India–Pakistan border, about 30 km from the city. High-energy, patriotic and packed — arrive early for a good seat.

Enquire to visit
04 · Partition Museum
Museum

Partition Museum

The world's first museum dedicated to the 1947 Partition, telling human stories through objects, letters and testimony. Housed in the beautifully restored historic Town Hall.

05 · Gobindgarh Fort
Heritage & culture

Gobindgarh Fort

An 18th-century fort turned living heritage space, with museums, a light-and-sound show, cultural performances and a food court. A great way to spend an evening.

06 · Durgiana Temple
Temple

Durgiana Temple

The "Silver Temple," a Hindu shrine to Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Vishnu whose layout beautifully echoes the Golden Temple, set around its own tranquil tank.

More to Explore

Beyond the headline sights.

Within the complex

Akal Takht

The highest seat of Sikh temporal authority, founded in 1606, facing the Golden Temple.
Cultural village

Sadda Pind

A recreated living Punjabi village — crafts, folk music and dance, and hearty local food.

Maharaja Ranjit Singh Museum

A former summer palace showcasing arms, paintings and artifacts of the Sikh Empire.

Mata Lal Devi Temple

The mirror-clad "Sheesh Mahal of Amritsar," with cave-like passages and shallow water crossings.

Khair-ud-Din Masjid & Khalsa College

A striking heritage mosque (1876) and the century-old Khalsa College, an architectural landmark.

Harike Wetland · day trip

The largest wetland in North India (~60 km); superb for birdwatching, especially in winter.

Tarn Taran Sahib · day trip

A historic gurudwara (~25 km) said to have the largest sarovar of any Sikh shrine.

Amritsari kulcha
Food & Cuisine

A pilgrimage for eaters.

Amritsar is as much a food capital as a spiritual one. Start with the iconic Amritsari kulcha — a crisp, tandoor-baked stuffed flatbread with chole and white butter — the city's signature morning ritual.

  • Amritsari fish fry — carom-spiced, gram-flour-battered, deep-fried.
  • Dal makhani — black lentils slow-cooked overnight, velvety and rich.
  • Thick lassi — topped with malai and butter, in giant glasses.
  • Sarson da saag & makki di roti — a winter classic.
  • Langar at the Golden Temple — free, 24 hours, the most moving meal in the city.

Iconic spots: Kesar Da Dhaba (est. 1916), Bharawan Da Dhaba, Brother's Dhaba — the Lawrence Road / Hall Gate area has the densest cluster of legendary eateries.

Hall Bazaar
Shopping

Phulkari, juttis and colour.

Amritsar's old bazaars are a riot of craft and colour. Come home with phulkari embroidery, handcrafted Punjabi juttis, bright textiles, woollens and brassware.
