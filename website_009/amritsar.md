## 1. About Amritsar

### Intro / positioning
Amritsar is the spiritual capital of Sikhism and the beating cultural heart of Punjab. Founded in 1577 by the fourth Sikh Guru, Guru Ram Das, the city takes its name from the **Amrit Sarovar** — the "Pool of Nectar" that surrounds the Golden Temple. It is a city of gold and devotion by day, of glowing reflections and shared meals by night, where faith, food, and history sit side by side.

### History & significance
- Grew around the sacred sarovar and the **Golden Temple (Sri Harmandir Sahib)**, the holiest shrine in Sikhism.
- Sat on historic trade routes, and this crossroads position shaped its now-legendary cuisine as travellers brought new ingredients and techniques.
- Flourished as the capital of the Sikh Empire under Maharaja Ranjit Singh.
- Carries deep imprints of India's freedom struggle — the **Jallianwala Bagh** massacre of 1919 — and of the 1947 **Partition**, memorialised today in the Partition Museum.

### Culture & vibe
- Famous Punjabi warmth and hospitality; a loud, generous, colourful energy.
- The spirit of **seva** (selfless service) and **langar** (the free community kitchen that feeds everyone, regardless of faith or status).
- **Festivals:** Baisakhi (~13–14 April, one of the biggest days in the Sikh calendar), Diwali / Bandi Chhor Divas, Lohri and Maghi (winter), and the Amritsar Sufi Festival.

### Quick facts
- **State:** Punjab, India
- **Best time to visit:** October–March
- **Ideal duration:** 2–3 days
- **Languages:** Punjabi, Hindi, English
- **Known for:** Golden Temple, Wagah Border ceremony, Amritsari kulcha
- **Airport code:** ATQ

### Best time to visit (seasonal)
- **Winter (Dec–Feb):** ~5–15°C — crisp, pleasant, the most enjoyable time for sightseeing; festive season.
- **Pleasant window (Oct–March):** peak tourist season, ideal weather overall.
- **Summer (Apr–Jul):** hot and dry, often 40°C+ (can spike higher) — plan indoor/early-morning visits.
- **Monsoon (Jun–Sep):** humid with heavy spells of rain; greener but sticky.

### How to reach
- **By air:** Sri Guru Ram Dass Jee International Airport (ATQ), well connected to major Indian cities and some international routes.
- **By rail:** Amritsar Junction, strongly connected across India, including frequent trains from Delhi.
- **By road:** ~450 km / roughly 8 hours from Delhi via NH44; good bus and cab connectivity.

## 2. Things to Do in Amritsar

> A comprehensive list of the city's marquee sights plus a few worthwhile day trips.

1. **Golden Temple (Sri Harmandir Sahib)** — The holiest shrine in Sikhism, its gilded sanctum mirrored in the sacred Amrit Sarovar. The 24-hour langar feeds 40,000+ people a day, free of charge. Utterly magical after dark when the gold glows against the water.
2. **Jallianwala Bagh** — A solemn memorial garden to the 1919 massacre, with preserved bullet marks and the martyrs' well. A five-minute walk from the Golden Temple.
3. **Wagah–Attari Border** — The theatrical evening "Beating Retreat" ceremony at the India–Pakistan border, about 30 km from the city. High-energy, patriotic, packed — arrive early for a good seat.
4. **Partition Museum** — The world's first museum dedicated to the 1947 Partition, telling human stories through objects, letters, and testimony. Housed in the historic Town Hall.
5. **Gobindgarh Fort** — An 18th-century fort turned living heritage space, with museums, a light-and-sound show, cultural performances, and a food court. Great for an evening.
6. **Durgiana Temple** — The "Silver Temple," a Hindu shrine to Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Vishnu whose layout beautifully echoes the Golden Temple.
7. **Sadda Pind** — A recreated living Punjabi village: crafts, folk music and dance, rural life demonstrations, and hearty local food. An easy, family-friendly cultural immersion.
8. **Maharaja Ranjit Singh Museum** — Set in a former summer palace, showcasing arms, paintings, and artifacts from the Sikh Empire.
9. **Akal Takht** — The highest seat of Sikh temporal authority (founded 1606), located within the Golden Temple complex.
10. **Mata Lal Devi Temple** — The mirror-clad "Sheesh Mahal of Amritsar," with cave-like passages and shallow water crossings; a quirky, popular pilgrimage stop.
11. **Hall Bazaar & Katra Jaimal Singh** — Historic, buzzing markets for phulkari, Punjabi juttis, and textiles (see Shopping below).
12. **Khair-ud-Din Masjid & Khalsa College** — A striking heritage mosque (1876) and the century-old Khalsa College, an architectural landmark.
13. **Harike Wetland (day trip)** — The largest wetland in North India (~60 km out); excellent for birdwatching, especially in winter.
14. **Tarn Taran Sahib (day trip)** — A historic gurudwara (~25 km) said to have the largest sarovar of any Sikh shrine.

### Food to try (callout)
Amritsar is a pilgrimage for eaters as much as for the devout.
- **Amritsari kulcha** with **chole** and white butter — a crisp, tandoor-baked stuffed flatbread; the city's signature morning ritual.
- **Amritsari fish fry** — carom-spiced, gram-flour-battered, deep-fried.
- **Dal makhani** — black lentils slow-cooked overnight, velvety and rich.
- **Pindi chole** — dark, dry chickpeas with pomegranate and black cardamom.
- **Thick lassi** topped with malai and butter, served in giant glasses.
- **Sarson da saag & makki di roti** — a winter classic.
- **Langar at the Golden Temple** — free, 24 hours, for everyone; the most moving meal in the city.
- **Iconic spots:** Kesar Da Dhaba (est. 1916), Bharawan Da Dhaba, Brother's Dhaba; the Lawrence Road / Hall Gate area has the densest cluster of legendary eateries.

### Shopping (callout)
- **What to buy:** phulkari embroidery, handcrafted Punjabi juttis, bright textiles and woollens, brassware.
- **Where:** Hall Bazaar (oldest and busiest), Katra Jaimal Singh (the home of authentic phulkari), and Kapda Bazaar (ethnic wear and suits).
